IRIS Sorting Study

IRIS is intended for use in the instrument reprocessing cycle. The system would automatically sort used instruments returned from the operating theatre, where they are often mixed together in cluttered trays.

The proposed workflow, and your part in it

The person at the sorting station is the operator. In this study, that is you.

  1. Before sorting

    1. You:Place the tray of instruments at the sorting station, next to an empty receiving tray.
    2. IRIS:Checks the tray with its camera against a list of instruments from one of multiple fixed sets, and shows what it found.
    3. You:Decide what to do if the tray does not match the set, then start sorting.

  2. While IRIS sorts

    1. IRIS:Moves each instrument into its position in the receiving tray.
    2. You:Stay at the station, within reach of the STOP button, until sorting ends. Alerts appear only on the screen.

  3. After sorting

    1. IRIS:Takes a photo of the receiving tray and labels each instrument in it.
    2. You:Check the result before the set moves to the next stage.

IRIS does not inspect instruments for damage or cleanliness.

Watch IRIS sort a tray

In the video, the operator follows this sequence:

  1. Presses Check the next tray. The camera finds the instruments on the input tray.
  2. Presses Start sorting. IRIS picks the instruments in the order it has selected.
  3. The arm moves each instrument into its slot in the receiving tray, one at a time.
  4. When all are placed, the camera photographs the receiving tray. The operator checks that each instrument is labelled correctly, in the right slot.

Three short tasks

In tasks 1 and 2 you will use a proposed new design of the operator's screen, opened at different moments. Do what you would do at work, then press Return to questions, whether or not you finished the task. Task 3 shows an example result photo to review.

We are testing the design, not your performance. If you are unsure what to do or cannot complete a task, that is useful feedback.

The screen works best on a computer or tablet. On a phone, use in landscape mode.
