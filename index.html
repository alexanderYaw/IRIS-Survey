IRIS sorting study

IRIS is a prototype robotic system designed to sort surgical instruments from an input tray into designated positions in a receiving tray. This study asks for your feedback on the proposed workflow and operator screen.

Where IRIS would be used

IRIS would work in the CSSD, at a sorting station where a set is prepared. We have not decided whether this would be when instruments arrive, before washing, or after washing, during inspection and assembly. One of the questions asks what you think.

The proposed workflow, and your part in it

The person at the sorting station is the operator. In this study, that is you.

  1. You:Place the tray of instruments at the sorting station, next to an empty receiving tray.
  2. IRIS:Checks the tray with its camera against the list of instruments in the set, and shows what it found.
  3. You:Answer any questions from IRIS, decide what to do if the tray does not match the set, then start sorting.
  4. IRIS:Moves each instrument into its position in the receiving tray.
  5. You:Stay at the station, within reach of the STOP button, until sorting ends. Alerts appear only on the screen.
  6. IRIS:Takes a photo of the receiving tray and labels each instrument in it.
  7. You:Check the result before the set moves to the next stage.

IRIS does not inspect instruments for damage or cleanliness; that stays with staff.

Watch IRIS sort a tray

The operator's screenThe robot arm

In the video, the operator follows the standard steps:

  1. Presses Check the next tray. The camera finds the instruments on the input tray.
  2. Presses Start sorting. IRIS picks the instruments in an order that does not disturb the pile.
  3. The arm moves each instrument into its slot in the receiving tray, one at a time.
  4. When all are placed, the camera photographs the receiving tray. The operator checks that each instrument is labelled correctly, in the right slot.

Most of the video plays at 1.5× speed.

Three short tasks

In tasks 1 and 2 you will use a proposed new design of the operator's screen, opened at different moments. Do what you would do at work, then press Return to questions, whether or not you finished the task. Task 3 shows a photo from the screen to review.

We are testing the design, not your performance. If you are unsure what to do or cannot complete a task, that is useful feedback.

The screen works best on a computer or tablet. On a phone, turn it sideways.

About the photo you just reviewed

Two labels in this photo were wrong on purpose.

The tissue forceps in slot 2 were labelled “Artery forceps”, and the artery forceps in slot 4 were labelled “Tissue forceps”. We did this to see whether the photo helps people catch a mistake.
